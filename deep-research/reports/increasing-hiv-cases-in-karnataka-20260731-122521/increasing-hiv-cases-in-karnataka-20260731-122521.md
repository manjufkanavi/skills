# Increasing HIV Cases in Karnataka: A Deep Dive

**Topic:** Why HIV/AIDS cases are rising sharply in Karnataka, who is affected, and what the state is doing about it.
**Date:** 31 July 2026 (re-run)

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Karnataka is witnessing a worrying surge in HIV/AIDS cases, concentrated among young adults. Official data from the Karnataka State AIDS Prevention Society (KSAPS) shows active HIV cases rose from **44,581 in 2023-24 to 66,606 in 2025-26** — a jump of nearly **50% in three years**. Transmission linked to male-to-male sexual contact (MSM) more than doubled from 166 to 412 cases over the same period, and a 2024 study found MSM-linked infections rising **17% year-on-year**, with Bengaluru recording the highest incidence among all Indian MSM sites. The state also estimates **over 56,000 people live with HIV without knowing it** — the highest such figure among Indian states — making early detection a public health priority. In response, Karnataka has ordered college health camps, expanded awareness via 442 Red Ribbon Clubs, and promoted digital tools like the BreakFree app and the 1097 helpline. Crucially, KSAPS has refuted viral claims that "7,000 students" are HIV-positive and stressed that testing remains voluntary under the HIV and AIDS (Prevention and Control) Act, 2017.

## 2. The Numbers: How Bad Is It?

- **Active HIV cases in Karnataka**: 44,581 (2023-24) → 62,664 (2024-25) → 66,606 (2025-26).
- **Age group 18–35**: HIV-positive individuals rose from ~44,500 (2023-24) to ~66,600 (2025-26); people aged 15–35 living with HIV grew from ~49,000 to ~83,000.
- **MSM cases** (men who have sex with men): 166 (2023-24) → 362 (2024-25) → 412 (2025-26); active MSM population reached under targeted interventions grew from 44,581 to 66,606.
- **Incidence trend**: a 2024 Journal of the International AIDS Society study across eight Indian clinic sites found HIV incidence among MSM rose 17% annually; Bengaluru had the highest rate at **3.5 infections per 100 person-years** (Belgaum lowest at 0.4).
- **Hidden burden**: **56,406 people** estimated to be unaware of their HIV-positive status — the highest among all Indian states; only about **12%** of HIV-positive individuals voluntarily walk into testing centres.
- **Overall burden**: ~2.6 lakh people live with HIV in Karnataka (third-highest in India per NACO's *Sankalak 2024*); ~2.05 lakh are on antiretroviral therapy (ART); adult HIV prevalence is **0.44%**, above the national average of ~0.2%.
- **Fact check**: the viral claim that ~7,000 Karnataka students are HIV-positive is **false**. KSAPS does not maintain student-specific data and called the figure "false and misleading"; it has recorded ~39,000 HIV-positive people cumulatively in the 18–35 group since 2004.

## 3. Why Are Cases Rising?

Health officials and experts point to several overlapping drivers:

1. **Dating apps and anonymous encounters** — Many partners, especially in MSM networks, meet via dating apps; anonymity makes partner notification and contact tracing extremely difficult. This is acute in Bengaluru's highly mobile population.
2. **Inconsistent condom use** — Low and inconsistent condom use among young people remains a leading driver of transmission.
3. **Low awareness and risk perception** — Many young people don't perceive themselves at risk and know little about STIs beyond HIV; misconceptions about HIV transmission persist.
4. **Rise in other STIs** — Syphilis, gonorrhoea and chlamydia are increasing; untreated STIs raise the risk of acquiring or transmitting HIV, and antibiotic-resistant gonorrhoea is a growing concern.
5. **Hostel and institutional outbreaks** — KSAPS Project Director Padma Basavanthappa attributes part of the rise to outbreaks in hostels, including cases involving men who had intercourse with female sex workers and then among themselves.
6. **Stigma around testing** — Fear of stigma delays testing; only ~12% test voluntarily, keeping the hidden burden large.
7. **High-burden districts** — A Cureus study of Vijayapura, Bagalkot and Belagavi found HIV concentrated among males, daily-wage workers, people with multiple partners and adults aged 25–49, with late referrals and loss-to-follow-up on ART.

## 4. What Is the Government Doing?

- **College health camps** — Following KSAPS recommendations, VTU and the Department of Collegiate Education are organising Integrated Health Camps (IHCs) in government, aided and private engineering/degree colleges. **Testing remains voluntary** — mandatory testing is prohibited under the HIV and AIDS (Prevention and Control) Act, 2017, which requires informed consent.
- **95-95-99 targets** — KSAPS's *Mobilisation for AIDS Suraksha (MAS)* campaign pushes the first "95" — making 95% of people aware of their HIV status — as the key to prevention, early detection and epidemic control.
- **BreakFree app** — A confidential self-risk assessment tool with a QR code linking users to nearby testing centres and counsellors.
- **Red Ribbon Clubs** — 442 clubs across Karnataka (40+ in Bengaluru) educate youth on prevention, sexuality and healthy lifestyles.
- **1097 helpline**, workplace awareness campaigns and testing camps lasting up to two months.
- **Targeted interventions for key populations** — NGOs working with MSM, transgender people, female sex workers, migrants and truckers; services include condom distribution, health check-ups and linkage to social security schemes.

## 5. Key Takeaways

- HIV in Karnataka is no longer declining — it is climbing fast among the young, driven by digital dating culture, inconsistent condom use and low testing uptake.
- The true burden is undercounted: over 56,000 people don't know their status, and only ~12% test voluntarily.
- The response is shifting from awareness posters to app-based risk assessment, college camps and NGO outreach — while testing stays voluntary and stigma-free by law.
- Experts urge reaching young people on the digital platforms they actually use, instead of blaming them.
