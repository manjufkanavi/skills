Increasing HIV Cases in Karnataka

Deep Research Report — Why cases are rising, who is affected, and what the state is doing

31 July 2026 | Sources: 53 | Queries: 26 | Rounds: 3

The Numbers

  • Active HIV cases: 44,581 (2023-24) → 62,664 (2024-25) → 66,606 (2025-26) — nearly +50% in three years.
  • 18–35 age group: ~44,500 (2023-24) → ~66,600 (2025-26); 15–35 living with HIV rose from ~49,000 to ~83,000.
  • MSM cases: 166 → 362 → 412 in two years; MSM incidence rising 17% year-on-year.
  • Bengaluru has the highest MSM incidence among all Indian sites: 3.5 per 100 person-years.
  • Adult HIV prevalence in Karnataka is 0.44% vs national average ~0.2%.

The Hidden Burden

  • 56,406 people estimated to be living with HIV without knowing it — the highest among Indian states.
  • Only about 12% of HIV-positive individuals test voluntarily.
  • ~2.6 lakh people live with HIV in Karnataka — third-highest in India (NACO Sankalak 2024).
  • ~2.05 lakh are on antiretroviral therapy (ART).
  • Fact check: claims that "7,000 students" are HIV-positive are false — KSAPS does not track student-specific data.

Why Are Cases Rising?

  • Dating apps & anonymous encounters — anonymity breaks partner notification and contact tracing, especially in MSM networks and Bengaluru's mobile population.
  • Inconsistent condom use and low risk perception among youth.
  • Rising syphilis, gonorrhoea and chlamydia — untreated STIs raise HIV risk; antibiotic-resistant gonorrhoea is a growing worry.
  • Hostel and institutional outbreaks, per KSAPS Project Director Padma Basavanthappa.
  • Stigma keeps people away from testing until late — only ~12% test voluntarily.

Government Response

  • College health camps — Integrated Health Camps (IHCs) in engineering/degree colleges via VTU and Collegiate Education; testing stays voluntary (HIV Act 2017 requires informed consent).
  • 95-95-99 targets via the MAS campaign — first priority: 95% of people aware of their HIV status.
  • BreakFree app — confidential self-risk assessment + QR links to testing centres and counsellors.
  • 442 Red Ribbon Clubs (40+ in Bengaluru) for youth awareness; 1097 helpline; workplace and two-month testing camps.
  • Targeted NGO interventions for MSM, transgender people, sex workers, migrants and truckers.

Key Takeaways

  • HIV in Karnataka is no longer declining — it is climbing fast among the young, driven by digital dating culture, inconsistent condom use and low testing uptake.
  • The true burden is undercounted: over 56,000 people don't know their status.
  • The response is shifting from posters to app-based risk assessment, college camps and NGO outreach — while keeping testing voluntary and stigma-free.
  • Experts urge reaching young people on the digital platforms they actually use, instead of blaming them.
