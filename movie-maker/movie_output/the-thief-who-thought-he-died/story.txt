In a quiet town lived a Brahmin known far and wide as a thief.

Four Brahmins arrived to sell their wares, earning a small purse of coins.

The thief slipped in as a friend, quoting the sacred texts with honeyed words.

"Your wisdom shines like the morning sun, honoured sages." — Thief Brahmin

Trusting him, they made him their travelling helper and guide.

"You shall be our helper on this journey, good sir." — Head Brahmin

They sold all their goods and bought glittering jewels with the coins.

Cutting their thighs, they hid the jewels inside and healed the wounds with ointment.

The thief saw everything and locked the hiding place in his mind.

"(to himself) The jewels are sewn into their very flesh. Tonight, I take them all." — Thief Brahmin

That night by the fire, the wise Head Brahmin read the thief's greedy heart.

"(aside) This one plots harm. Let us teach him a lesson he will never forget." — Head Brahmin

They slipped a sleeping draught into his food, and he sank into deep sleep.

They bound him, dragged him to the town gate, and left him in a muddy ditch.

Waking, half-deaf with fear, he swore he had died and been judged for his crimes.

"(waking in terror) I am dead! The gods have punished me for my theft!" — Thief Brahmin

From that day he never stole again, for he feared the truth of his own soul.
