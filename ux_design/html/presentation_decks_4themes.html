Design Thinking 02 / 08

The Five Stages

Design thinking is non-linear and iterative

Empathize

Research your users' needs. Observe, engage, and absorb to understand their experience and motivations.

Define

Synthesize findings into a human-centered problem statement. Focus on the real need, not the symptoms.

Ideate

Challenge assumptions and explore radical solutions. Quantity over quality at this stage.

Prototype

Make ideas tangible. Low-fidelity models help you learn fast and fail cheap.

Test

Put prototypes in front of users. Every test teaches you something new about your solution.

Design Thinking 03 / 08

Core Principles

What makes design thinking effective

  • User-centered, not technology-driven
  • Embrace ambiguity and exploration
  • Bias toward action over analysis
  • Show, don't tell — make ideas visible
  • Iterate fast, fail early, learn always
  • Radical collaboration across disciplines
  • Optimize for learning, not delivery
  • Design for the edge, not the average

Art of Simplicity 04 / 08

Typography as Structure

Let type do the heavy lifting

Large

Headlines grab attention. Use a single font family with bold weight for hierarchy. No icons needed.

Medium

Subheads create sections. Regular weight with generous spacing between lines.

Small

Body text should be comfortable to read at 10-12pt. Emphasize through weight, not color.

The key is restraint. One typeface. Two weights. Generous line height. The type hierarchy replaces the need for decorative borders, icons, and color blocks.

Bold carries the message. The rest follows in quiet support. This is the essence of typographic minimalism.

Art of Simplicity 06 / 08

Rules of Minimalism

A practical checklist

  • One type family per design
  • One accent color — no more
  • Every element must serve a purpose
  • Use space as a design tool, not a byproduct
  • Let content breathe with generous padding
  • Typography alone should create hierarchy
  • Align everything — even asymmetry needs alignment

The Growth Framework

Three engines of sustainable growth

Acquisition

Content marketing, SEO, partnerships. Attract users through value, not noise.

Activation

Onboarding, first-value moment, habit formation. Convert visitors into engaged users.

Retention

Feature velocity, support quality, community. Keep users coming back through delight.

Revenue

Pricing strategy, upsell paths, packaging. Monetize value without friction.

Referral

Product virality, network effects, ambassador programs. Turn users into advocates.

Expansion

New markets, new features, new segments. Grow beyond your current ceiling.

Growth & Strategy 03 / 08

Unit Economics

Know your numbers

CAC
Cost to Acquire
LTV
Lifetime Value
3:1
LTV to CAC Ratio

Payback Period

Target under 12 months. Every month of delay burns cash and extends the path to profitability.

Net Revenue Retention

Target over 110%. Expansion revenue should outpace churn — your existing customers should grow.

Growth & Strategy 04 / 08

Team Scaling

Building the right organization

Principle: Span of Control

Aim for 5-8 direct reports. Beyond that, information flow breaks down and decisions slow.

Principle: Team Autonomy

Small teams, clear ownership, decision rights at the edge. Centralize only what needs consistency.

Principle: Hire for Growth

Each hire should make the team 10x better, not just add headcount. Culture compounds.

Growth & Strategy 05 / 08

Key Metrics

What to measure and why

  • MRR / ARR — Monthly and annual recurring revenue
  • Churn Rate — Percentage of customers lost per period
  • Activation Rate — Percentage reaching the "aha" moment
  • NPS — Net Promoter Score as a proxy for loyalty
  • CAC Payback — Months to recover acquisition cost
